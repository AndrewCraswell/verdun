# Commissioning, Customization, AI Generation and Achievable Art Direction for a Solo-Dev Browser WW1 (Verdun 1916) Game

Research date: 2026-10-03. Every price below is in USD unless noted, is dated to its source, and should be treated as an estimate. Many rate sources are outsourcing vendors (Rocketbrush, VSQUAD) or tool vendors / affiliates (Meshy, Tripo, Neural4D, Ziva, Cinevva), so they have commercial bias; this is flagged inline.

## 1. Commissioning: what do realistic game-ready characters, props and environment kits cost in 2025-2026, where do you hire, and what goes in the contract?

### Takeaway
A rigged, game-ready, realistic character from a mid-level freelancer or Eastern European studio typically runs about $1,000-$5,000 and takes 3-6 weeks. "Photoreal / studio-grade" heroes run $4,000-$20,000+. Weapons run about $1,500-$3,000 at FPS-level detail, and vehicles about $4,000-$8,000+. For a top-down browser game you can spec far lower detail (no facial rig, no first-person weapon views), which pushes costs toward the bottom of those ranges. Contracts must include a written work-for-hire clause plus a present-tense ("hereby assigns") copyright assignment, a moral-rights waiver and an originality warranty, all signed before work starts.

### Cited Findings
**Character rates (2025-2026)**
- VSQUAD (vendor), updated 2026-09-11, gives these 3D character price bands: basic $1,000-$5,000; stylized mid-core $2,000-$5,000; stylized or cinematic AAA $2,500-$20,000; photorealistic or studio-level $4,000-$20,000+. Concept and art direction add $200-$1,500+. Rush deadlines add 25-50%. Hair and fur grooming is "one of the most expensive items in the pipeline." — [VSQUAD, "How much does a 3D character cost" (updated Sep 2026)](https://vsquad.art/blog/how-much-does-a-3d-character-cost-vsquad)
- VSQUAD lists the pipeline stages that drive cost: concept, high-poly sculpt, retopology, UV and bake, PBR texturing, skeletal and facial rigging, and optimization/export. "The more detailed the model, the more time is required for sculpting, retopology, and baking." — [VSQUAD (Sep 2026)](https://vsquad.art/blog/how-much-does-a-3d-character-cost-vsquad)
- Hourly rates by experience (VSQUAD, Sep 2026): junior freelancer $15-$30/h; mid-level $30-$80/h; senior $50-$150+/h; studio or agency $50-$200+/h. — [VSQUAD (Sep 2026)](https://vsquad.art/blog/how-much-does-a-3d-character-cost-vsquad)
- Hourly rates by region (Rocketbrush, vendor, 2025): a North American or Western European specialist charges $80-$150/h; an Eastern European studio $40-$70/h; a Southeast Asian freelancer $15-$40/h. Their worked example: "a realistic character taking 50 hours at $40/hr would cost you around $2,000." A game-ready rigged character costs "$1,000 to $5,000+" with a "3 to 6 weeks" timeline. This comes from search-result snippets of Rocketbrush pages; I did not fetch the full pages. — [Rocketbrush, 3D modeling cost 2025](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/how-much-does-3d-modeling-cost-rates-and-budget-management-tips); [Rocketbrush, 3D character art prices guide](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/3d-character-art-prices-guide)
- Forum and individual-artist data points (search snippets, undated, attribution to a specific thread not verified):
  - One studio quote: a full character from scratch "starts at $5,000 USD minimum," including 2 outfits and full rigging.
  - Some individual artists list simple or detailed characters at about $300 and $500, with animation-ready rigging +$250. Low-poly characters start at €250+.
  - A BlenderArtists commenter: "photo-realistic character models would easily cost well north of $1000," estimating "a day or two to sculpt, a few days to texture, and a few days to rig."
  - Sources: [BlenderArtists, "Commissioning Character Model"](https://blenderartists.org/t/commissioning-character-model/1354633); [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2675157)

**Props, weapons, vehicles, environment**
- Rocketbrush (vendor, Sep 2025): game-ready weapon $1,500-$3,000; optimized vehicle $4,000-$8,000+. "Simple daggers require 2-3 days; intricate tanks demand weeks." Moving parts and animation raise the cost significantly. Mobile needs low-poly versions, while AAA PC expects ultra-detailed models. Their studio rate is $35-$37/h, "with reduced per-asset costs on bulk orders." — [Rocketbrush, 3D weapon & vehicle art price](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/3d-weapon-vehicle-art-price-what-studios-should-expect)
- From Polycount search snippets: elaborate props generally range $100-$1,000, bulk contracts for similar assets bring the per-asset cost to about $500, and an "FPS gun-complexity prop" is about $1,160 at entry-level industry rates. These are undated and the specific thread was not verified. — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/1233168)
- Fiverr has many "game-ready hard-surface / weapons" gigs that include clean topology, UVs, PBR and source files. Examples: [Fiverr gig 1](https://fiverr.com/novameshcreativ/create-a-game-ready-assets), [Fiverr gig 2](https://fiverr.com/harsh3d/create-game-ready-weapons)
- I found no reliable 2025-2026 per-module price for a modular trench or environment kit (see Gaps).

**Where to hire, and platform fees**

Each new contract also has a one-time initiation fee of $0.99-$14.99. — [goLance, "Upwork fees explained 2026"](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-fees-explained-2026)
- Fiverr (2026): buyers pay a 5.5% service fee plus a $3.50 flat fee on orders under $200, and sellers pay a 20% commission. **Rights and contracts (US law)**
- Paying for a work does not by itself transfer copyright. Copyright vests in the creator unless there is a written work-for-hire arrangement or an assignment. — [Game Developer, "The real meaning of work for hire"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-real-meaning-of-work-for-hire); [GameDev.net, Untangling licensing and copyright in game art](https://www.gamedev.net/tutorials/business/business-and-law/untangling-licensing-and-copyright-in-game-art-r3144)
- Contractor work is work-for-hire only if it falls in one of the nine statutory categories (17 U.S.C. §101) and there is a signed writing. Video games are "audiovisual works," so integrated game art can qualify. Standalone deliverables may not. The article recommends a 5-part clause set:
  1. Work-for-hire designation
  2. A present-tense "hereby assigns all rights" backup assignment
  3. Further assurances
  4. VARA / moral-rights waiver
  5. An originality and non-infringement warranty

  Sign before work begins, because retroactive agreements cannot create work-for-hire status. Assignments (unlike true work-for-hire) are subject to Section 203 termination after 35 years. — [Legal Moves Law Firm, Work-for-Hire for Game Studios (Jun 2026)](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/work-for-hire-agreement/)
- Work-for-hire contracts commonly still allow the artist to show the work in their portfolio and get game credit. — [Game Developer, "The real meaning of work for hire"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-real-meaning-of-work-for-hire)

### Inferences
- **Spec to the camera.** For an angled top-down browser game, commission "mid-poly soldier, body-only skeleton, no facial blendshapes, 1-2 texture sets, hands/feet compatible with a standard humanoid rig." That removes the most expensive line items VSQUAD names (facial rig, hair grooming, extreme sculpt detail). A realistic but RTS-scale soldier from a mid-level freelancer plausibly lands around $800-$2,500 for the first base, with uniform or role variants at a fraction of that. This is my estimate from the hourly and per-character ranges above, not a quoted price.
- **Commission a system, not individual characters.** Order one base body plus a modular uniform kit (tunic/greatcoat, puttees, webbing, helmet, pack) on one shared skeleton. Then order variants for French poilu (horizon blue, Adrian helmet) and German infantry (feldgrau, Stahlhelm/Pickelhaube). Variants then cost hours rather than weeks.
- **Example "minimum cohesive Verdun set" budget (my estimate):**
  - 2 faction base soldiers: about $2.5k each
  - 6-8 role/uniform variants: about $300-$600 each
  - About 10 weapons at top-down detail: about $300-$800 each
  - About 40 props: about $100-$300 each
  - A modular trench/shell-crater kit of about 30 modules: about $150-$400 each
  - 2-3 artillery pieces: about $1.5k-$4k each
  - Total: roughly $25k-$60k over 3-6 months with 2-3 freelancers working in parallel. Animation (mocap packs or Mixamo/ActorCore) is extra.
  - All of this is an inference from the rate bands above.
- **Hiring channels** (not verified this session):
  - Polycount's freelance/job forum, ArtStation Jobs, and r/gameDevClassifieds and r/INAT are where realistic-PBR specialists are found most directly, with no platform fee.
  - Fiverr Pro and Upwork add 3-10% client fees but give escrow and dispute handling.
  - CGTrader and TurboSquid sellers who already sell WW1 models are good candidates for "custom variant" commissions, because they own reusable base meshes.
- **Exclusivity.** If you buy a seller's existing model and ask for edits, you usually get a non-exclusive license, not ownership. Exclusivity needs explicit contract language and costs more.

### Gaps
- I found no neutral (non-vendor) 2025-2026 survey of character-art rates. All bands come from outsourcing vendors or forum anecdotes.
- I found no published per-module price for modular trench or WW1 environment kits.
- I did not verify current ArtStation Jobs, Polycount forum or r/gameDevClassifieds posting rules or rate threads, or CGTrader "custom request" fees, this session.
- Contract guidance is US-specific. I found no source on EU or UK moral-rights rules (which cannot always be waived) for cross-border contractors.

## 2. Customization: Character Creator, Marvelous Designer, Substance Painter and MetaHuman for WW1 uniforms

### Takeaway
Reallusion Character Creator 5 ($299 perpetual, Windows only, released Aug 2025) is the most practical kitbash base. WW1 French content already exists on its marketplace ("Great War French soldier outfit," Adrian helmet 1916, Brodie Mk1). Store content needs the separate Export License to ship in a game, priced by default at 1.5x the iContent price. Marvelous Designer is $39/mo or $280/yr, and a cheaper indie tier was added Jan 2026. Substance 3D Painter on Steam is a one-time ~£168 / ~$200. Epic's official position since June 2025 (UE 5.6) is that MetaHumans may be used in any engine, free under $1M revenue, but they are far heavier than a top-down browser game needs.

### Inferences
- **Horizon-blue conversion workflow.** Take a rigged WW1 base (or the CC5 Poilu outfit with Export License), then:
  1. Re-texture in Substance Painter using a fill layer, hue/sat-shifted base color and a "mud/wear" smart material stack.
  2. Fix silhouette-defining pieces (Adrian vs Stahlhelm vs Brodie helmets, greatcoat skirt, puttees) in Blender or Marvelous Designer.
  3. Export through AccuRIG/Mixamo to a shared skeleton.

  A solo dev learning the tools should expect weeks for the first faction and days for later variants (my estimate).
- **Uniform fidelity matters less than shape.** At top-down distance, faction recognition comes from helmet shape and uniform hue more than from cloth folds. Marvelous Designer is most valuable for one or two greatcoat sims that you then bake into low-poly meshes, not for every variant.
- **CC and MetaHuman are overkill by default.** Both produce high-poly characters with complex facial rigs, wasted on an RTS-scale browser camera. Expect to decimate or use the lowest LODs, strip facial bones and atlas textures. This applies especially to CC5's HD bases, which have "16x more mesh detail."
- **Year-one tooling estimate:** CC5 $299 + MD $280/yr (or indie tier) + Substance Painter about $200 + Blender (free) + AccuRIG (free), so roughly $800-$1,000. Store content with Export Licenses comes on top.

- The Marvelous Designer indie price and revenue threshold are not public.
- I did not verify the MetaHuman Creator web-app shutdown date with Epic.

## 3. AI 3D generation in 2026: quality, rigging, topology, licensing and cost

### Takeaway
As of late 2026, Meshy, Tripo, Rodin (Hyper3D/Deemos) and Hunyuan3D are the live mainstream options at about $20-$30/month. CSM Cube shut down (Jan 2026, company acquired by Google) and Luma Genie is effectively gone. AI output is "genuinely production-usable" for static props and debris. Characters still need manual retopology because AI topology lacks animation edge loops. Raw AI output is not copyrightable in the US, so you cannot own it exclusively, and Steam requires disclosure of shipped AI content.

### Cited Findings
**Meshy**
- Free tier: 100 credits/month under CC BY 4.0, with Meshy retaining some rights. Pro tier: 1,000 credits/month, API access, and "Premium subscribers own all assets exclusively" (private assets). Studio and Enterprise tiers also exist. — [Meshy pricing (fetched Oct 2026)](https://www.meshy.ai/pricing)
- Pro is about $20/mo and Studio about $60/mo (third-party, 2026). — [Neural4D benchmark (2026, competitor)](https://www.neural4d.com/features/neural4d-vs-tripo-vs-meshy-vs-rodin)
- "Quad-dominant output with mostly clean UV unwraps." It needs cleanup on highly concave shapes. Humanoid and quadruped rigging come with 600+ animation presets. Exports FBX/USDZ with engine plugins. — [Ziva (Jun 2026)](https://ziva.sh/blogs/best-ai-3d-asset-generators); [Ludo.ai, Meshy alternatives (2026)](https://ludo.ai/compare/meshy-alternatives)
- Cinevva (updated Sep 14, 2026): Meshy 6/7 downloads require a paid subscription, and 10 "Lite" downloads per month are free. — [Cinevva (Sep 2026)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generators)

**Tripo**

Paid tiers have "private models, commercially use available." — [Neural4D (2026)](https://www.neural4d.com/features/neural4d-vs-tripo-vs-meshy-vs-rodin); [Costbench, Tripo pricing 2026](https://costbench.com/software/ai-3d-generation/tripo-ai/); [Cinevva (Sep 2026)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generators)

- Rigging supports 7 skeleton types with Mixamo bone naming, plus "Smart Mesh" low-poly output. Ziva calls it "noticeably better" than Meshy on hard-surface or mechanical assets. — [Ludo.ai (2026)](https://ludo.ai/compare/meshy-alternatives); [Ziva (Jun 2026)](https://ziva.sh/blogs/best-ai-3d-asset-generators)

**Rodin / Hyper3D (DeemosTech)**
- Free: pay-as-you-go at $1.50/credit. Creator: $30/mo ($24 annual), about 60 models/mo, unlimited private assets, HD textures. **Hunyuan3D (Tencent)**
- The hosted Hunyuan3D 3.0 launched globally in Nov 2025, and 3.1 followed in Jan 2026. These newest engines are "not open-sourced." The free tier is 20 generations per day, with text/image/sketch input and OBJ/GLB with PBR output. — [Cinevva (Sep 2026)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generators)

**Copyright and platform disclosure**
- The US Copyright Office holds that works "generated entirely by artificial intelligence are not copyrightable," and prompts alone do not count as authorship. Substantial manual modification and the compilation or arrangement of assets can be protected. — [Promise Legal, AI Game Assets in 2026 (Jul 2026)](https://blog.promise.legal/ai-game-assets-copyright-steam-disclosure-2026/)
- Steam (policy rewritten Jan 16, 2026) requires disclosure of pre-generated AI content that players consume. Behind-the-scenes efficiency tools are exempt. Disclosures appear on the store page. — [tbreak (2026)](https://tbreak.com/steam-ai-disclosure-policy-update-developers/); [Promise Legal (Jul 2026)](https://blog.promise.legal/ai-game-assets-copyright-steam-disclosure-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Best fit for a WW1 trench game:** use AI for the long tail of static clutter: ammo crates, sandbag variants, duckboards, barbed-wire pickets, broken carts, rubble, shell casings, stretchers, field telephones, debris piles. These are high-volume, low-hero-value, and rarely animated. One to three months of Meshy Pro and/or Tripo Pro (about $20-$60) plus Hunyuan3D's free daily quota can produce hundreds of candidates. The real cost is cleanup time: decimating to browser budgets, re-UVing or atlasing, and re-texturing to a shared palette (my estimate).
- **Not recommended for final soldiers.** Use AI for concept, blockout or reference instead, because of the retopology/edge-loop problem and the importance of a consistent shared skeleton. Meshy and Tripo auto-rigging can work for background, non-deforming-critical units, but quality should be tested per asset.
- **Ownership.** Use paid tiers only, for private assets and clear commercial terms. Assume no exclusive copyright on lightly edited AI output. Avoid self-hosted Hunyuan3D 2.x if you or your company are in the EU, UK or South Korea. TRELLIS.2 (MIT) is the cleanest self-hosted option.
- **Disclosure.** If the game ships on Steam (even as a desktop wrapper of the browser build), AI-generated shipped art must be disclosed. Browser portals' policies were not checked.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous, independent, dated benchmark of AI tools specifically for humanoid characters in period uniforms. Comparison sources are vendors or affiliates.
- Per-generation credit costs (text-to-3D vs image-to-3D vs texture vs rig vs remesh) for Meshy and Tripo were not visible on the fetched pages.
- I could not confirm Tripo's current full plan list directly (403).
- Browser game portals' (CrazyGames, Poki, itch.io) AI-content policies were not researched.

## 4. Art direction references: how existing WW1 (and WW2 top-down) games look, what they cost and which looks a solo dev can reach

### Takeaway
"Realistic" WW1 games range from tiny-studio Unity shooters (Verdun, Tannenberg, Isonzo) that earn authenticity through historical research of uniforms, weapons and terrain rather than budget, up to multi-million-dollar AAA (Battlefield 1). The closest reference for an angled top-down browser game is Foxhole: a small team (started with 7, one modeler) in UE4 with PBR "miniature" realism tuned for top-down readability. Company of Heroes-class RTS realism used about 4,000-triangle infantry. Stylized WW1 games (11-11 Memories Retold, Valiant Hearts) show that painterly or 2D treatments can carry the subject's emotion with far less asset fidelity.

### Cited Findings
**Verdun, Tannenberg and Isonzo (M2H / Blackmill Games)**
- Verdun was released on Steam Apr 28, 2015 after more than a year of Early Access. It was developed by M2H and Blackmill Games (Alkmaar, Netherlands). — [Wikipedia, Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdun_(video_game))
- The "tiny studios" traveled to battlefields across France and Belgium to recreate them 1:1, and studied diaries and photographs for uniforms and weapons. Cliqist contrasts this with Battlefield 1's "multi-million dollar" production but gives no figures. — [Cliqist (Jun 2017)](https://cliqist.com/2017/06/26/little-indie-verdun-stands-multi-million-dollar-battlefield-1/)
- The WW1 Game Series (Verdun, Tannenberg, Isonzo) was produced "in collaboration with Unity and Firelight Technologies (FMOD)." Historical accuracy is a "central pillar," with uniforms, weapon models and landscapes recreated from research. Blackmill "work with people from around the world." Isonzo took about 2 years. — [WW1 Game Series, About](https://www.ww1gameseries.com/about/); [Blackmill Games](https://www.blackmillgames.com/)

**Foxhole (Siege Camp): the closest top-down analogue**
- Foxhole is built in Unreal Engine 4 with an axonometric, RTS-like top-down view. — [Wikipedia, Foxhole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxhole_(video_game))
- Production and art notes from Sketchfab (Jul 16, 2018):
  - The team started with 7 people, including "one modeler, one level designer, and one animator."
  - The artists were trained in traditional animation.
  - They adopted Substance Painter/Designer for consistent PBR.
  - The look aims to capture how "tabletop miniatures" feel.
  - The soldier is deliberately generic: "a blank slate in which you can add your own meaning."
  - They tuned "the shape language, the level of detail, the amount of noise, and the on screen size of objects," prioritizing "objects reading clearly as opposed to being noisy and muddy."
  - Materials should "read as metals" or "read as wood," with paint over base metal and weathering on top.
  - Materials are abstracted into "clearly discernible representations of their real world counterparts" rather than photorealism.

  Source: [Sketchfab, Game Studio Spotlight: Clapfoot (Jul 2018)](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/game-studio-spotlight-clapfoot/)

**Company of Heroes (Relic)**
- Company of Heroes "features 4000 triangle characters." This was notably high for RTS units of its era. Generic RTS guidance on the same forums is under about 1,000-1,500 tris when 30-40 soldiers are on screen, with LOD chains of about 3k down to 1-2k. These come from forum snippets, mostly older threads. — [Polycount RTS polycount discussion](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/688734); [SpringRTS forum, normal polycount](https://springrts.com/phpbb/viewtopic.php?p=215280)
- Company of Heroes 3's art direction (2022 dev diary):
  - Art Director Tristan Brett set three pillars: inspiration, style and subjects.
  - Influences include the films *Fury* and *Dunkirk*.
  - Realistic dirt.
  - Units that react to their environment by changing stance when planes pass or enemies are close.
  - Division-specific patches and insignia.

  Sources: [GamingBolt, CoH3 art & authenticity dev diary](https://gamingbolt.com/company-of-heroes-3-developer-diary-discusses-art-and-authenticity-including-realistic-dirt); [TheSixthAxis (Apr 2022)](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/04/22/company-of-heroes-3-dev-diary-trailer-art-and-authenticity/)

**Stylized WW1 references**
- 11-11 Memories Retold (Aardman + DigixArt, for Bandai Namco, 2018): each frame is built from "millions of individual brush strokes" that move and dissolve. Influences are Monet and Turner, chosen because impressionism conveys WW1's emotions. Atomhawk helped replicate the expressionistic painterly style. — [Engadget (Nov 2018)](https://www.engadget.com/2018-11-15-11-11-memories-retold-paint-art-aardman.html); [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/wordpress/top-stories/aardman-bandai-namco-craft-painterly-wwi-narrative-11-11-memories-retold)
- Valiant Hearts: The Great War (Ubisoft Montpellier) runs on the UbiArt Framework, a 2.5D engine for 2D animated vector art. — [Wikipedia, Valiant Hearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valiant_Hearts:_The_Great_War); [Wikipedia, UbiArt Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UbiArt_Framework)

**Other realistic multiplayer references**
- Beyond the Wire is a WW1 multiplayer shooter "with an emphasis on realism" by Redstone Interactive (British Columbia, Canada). — [Giant Bomb, Redstone Interactive](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Companies/Redstone_Interactive_Ltd)
- Holdfast: Nations at War (Anvil Game Studios) runs on Unity, with 150+ players per server. It is Napoleonic and WW1-adjacent and still receiving updates in Jul 2026. — [Giant Bomb, Holdfast](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Holdfast_Nations_At_War); [MassivelyOP (Jul 2026)](https://massivelyop.com/2026/07/04/mmo-pvp-shooter-holdfast-nations-at-war-adds-new-maps-new-mode-and-stick-weapons/)

### Inferences
- **Tiers of "realistic" for a solo dev**, roughly in order of achievability:
  1. **Foxhole-style "miniature realism."** PBR, generic but historically correct soldiers, strong material and shape readability, viewed from an angled top-down camera. This is the most achievable with bought, kitbashed and AI assets plus one or two commissioned base characters, and it is the best match for a browser.
  2. **Company of Heroes / Men of War / Gates of Hell-style close-zoom RTS realism.** Unit detail (insignia, kit), destruction, environmental reactions and cinematic VFX at about 4k-tri infantry. This is achievable in look-alike stills, but the animation-reaction systems and VFX polish are the expensive part, not the meshes.
  3. **Verdun/Isonzo first-person realism.** Requires FPS-level weapon and hand fidelity and close-up faces. Not recommended for a top-down browser game, and it multiplies asset cost (Rocketbrush puts FPS weapons at $1.5k-$3k each).
  4. **Battlefield 1 photorealism.** Out of reach.
- **Stylized alternatives** (11-11 painterly, Valiant Hearts 2D) show that a strongly authored look lowers the per-asset fidelity bar. The catch is that a custom painterly shader is a technical-art project in its own right.
- **Budget scale.** Verdun's team being "tiny" and still reaching recognized authenticity suggests that historical research (uniform colors, helmet shapes, trench construction, terrain) carries "realism" more than polygon budget does.

### Gaps
- I found no team-size, budget or polycount data for Verdun, Tannenberg, Isonzo, Beyond the Wire, Holdfast, Gates of Hell or Men of War. The Blackmill interview page found (XboxAddict) is now offline.
- I found no published character polycounts for Foxhole, Company of Heroes 3 or Gates of Hell. The 4,000-tri figure is for the original Company of Heroes, from a forum.
- There are no public budget figures for Battlefield 1 or 11-11 Memories Retold.

## 5. Middle-ground "realistic-stylized" directions: hiding asset inconsistency, and what reads as "real" from an angled top-down camera

### Takeaway
A shared post-processing stack (color grade/LUT, fog, bloom), a restricted muted palette and re-tinted materials are the cheapest way to make mixed bought, AI and commissioned assets look like one game. From an angled top-down camera, realism comes from silhouettes, material readability, animation and reactions, lighting and atmospheric VFX (smoke, mud, shell bursts), not from facial detail.

### Cited Findings
- Mixed asset packs clash through different texel densities, levels of stylization, color spaces and baked lighting. "Post-processing is the great equalizer — a distinctive grade, consistent fog and bloom, and maybe a stylization shader pushes diverse sources toward one look more cheaply than any retexturing." Wayline recommends:
  - Define a palette and level of detail before acquiring assets.
  - Tweak roughness and metallic values and re-color textures to the palette.
  - Unify lighting.
  - "Never use an asset straight out of the box."

  Sources: [Wayline, Developer checklist: fixing asset pack art clashes](https://www.wayline.io/blog/developer-checklist-fixing-asset-pack-art-clashes); [Wayline, Unity asset pack visual consistency](https://www.wayline.io/blog/unity-asset-pack-visual-consistency); [Bugnet, How to avoid the asset store look](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-avoid-the-asset-store-look)
- Foxhole tuned shape language, detail level, noise and on-screen size for top-down readability, preferring "objects reading clearly as opposed to being noisy and muddy." It used one consistent PBR layering logic: base material, then paint, then weathering. — [Sketchfab (Jul 2018)](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/game-studio-spotlight-clapfoot/)
- From the StarCraft II art lead's interview: RTS art is seen in a top-down three-quarter view, so silhouettes from that angle are made "very distinct and iconic." Detail is kept low, with "bigger shapes rather than small details like nuts and bolts," and characters must read from different directions and distances. Further search snippets note that small on-screen characters need exaggeration. — [Blizzplanet, Eurogamer interviews Samwise Didier](https://www.blizzplanet.com/blog/comments/eurogamer_interviews_samwise_didier); [Polycount, RTS woes](https://polycount.com/discussion/45185/rts-woes)
- Company of Heroes 3 put its realism effort into unit behaviour (stance changes in reaction to aircraft or nearby enemies), dirt and division insignia, with cinematic film references (*Fury*, *Dunkirk*). — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/company-of-heroes-3-developer-diary-discusses-art-and-authenticity-including-realistic-dirt)
- 11-11 Memories Retold chose painterly impressionism on purpose to express the war's emotions (sadness, fear), with "broad brush strokes, strong colors, and atmospheric lighting." — [Engadget (Nov 2018)](https://www.engadget.com/2018-11-15-11-11-memories-retold-paint-art-aardman.html); [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/aardman-animation-on-creating-its-first-console-game/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended direction: "muted documentary realism," a Foxhole-like miniature realism graded toward period photography.**
  - Desaturated earth palette: mud browns, chalk and grey, shattered-wood ochre. Faction colors (horizon blue vs feldgrau) are the only saturated accents, kept slightly lifted so they read against mud.
  - Low-sun or overcast lighting.
  - Height fog and drifting smoke volumes.
  - Shell-crater decals and wet-mud material blending.
  - A mild film grain or vignette pass.

  This hides texel-density and fidelity mismatches between assets and matches the historical photographic record of Verdun.
- **Readability rules for the angled top-down camera:**
  1. Faction is read by helmet silhouette plus uniform hue.
  2. Role is read by large-shape kit (MG tripod, stretcher, rifle and bayonet length, flamethrower tank) rather than insignia.
  3. Animation, posture and reactions (crouch-run, prone, flinch at shell bursts, wounded crawl) carry more "realism" than texture detail. Mocap libraries (ActorCore, Mixamo) are therefore high-value buys.
  4. VFX (artillery bursts, dirt plumes, smoke, gas clouds, muzzle flash) and lighting dominate perceived quality at this distance.
  5. Faces are almost irrelevant. Spend zero budget on facial rigs.
- **Browser budget hint** (inference, not researched here): CoH-era 4k-tri infantry is a useful ceiling. Squads of 30-40+ visible soldiers on WebGL/WebGPU likely need about 1.5k-4k tris per soldier with LODs, atlased textures and GPU instancing or skinned batching. A separate researcher should confirm engine budgets.
- **A painterly or ink-wash post shader** (lighter than 11-11's brush-stroke renderer) is a fallback that hides asset inconsistency even more aggressively. The cost is losing the "realistic" label.

### Gaps
- I found no published GDC-style talk or postmortem quantifying which factors (animation vs lighting vs mesh detail) drive perceived realism at RTS distances. The guidance here comes from developer interviews and indie-blog advice.
- Wayline and Bugnet are indie blogs of general advice, not authoritative studies.
- Browser-specific (WebGL/WebGPU) poly and draw-call budgets were not researched in this section.

## 6. Overall: which route mix is achievable for a solo browser-game developer?

### Takeaway
A hybrid is the realistic path:
- Buy and kitbash characters (CC5 plus WW1 content with Export License, or rigged WW1 soldiers from CGTrader/TurboSquid/RenderHub), re-tinted in Substance Painter.
- Commission one or two base soldiers or a modular uniform kit, and the signature trench kit, if budget allows (about $5k-$25k).
- Fill the prop long tail with AI (about $20-$60 per month of subscriptions).
- Unify everything with a muted, fog-heavy grade under a Foxhole-style top-down "miniature realism" direction.

### Cited Findings
- Character commission bands (rigged, game-ready) are $1k-$5k over 3-6 weeks, and photoreal $4k-$20k+. — [VSQUAD (Sep 2026)](https://vsquad.art/blog/how-much-does-a-3d-character-cost-vsquad); [Rocketbrush](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/3d-character-art-prices-guide)
- CC5 is $299, store content Export Licenses are about 1.5x the iContent price, and WW1 French content exists. — [CG Channel (Aug 2025)](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/08/reallusion-releases-character-creator-5/); [Reallusion forum](https://forum.reallusion.com/PrintTopic335621.aspx); [Reallusion Marketplace](https://marketplace.reallusion.com/great-war-french-soldier-outfit)
- AI subscriptions cost Meshy Pro about $20/mo, Tripo Pro $19.90/mo and Rodin Creator $30/mo. Props are production-usable; characters need retopology. — [Hyper3D pricing](https://hyper3d.ai/pricing); [Neural4D](https://www.neural4d.com/features/neural4d-vs-tripo-vs-meshy-vs-rodin); [Cinevva (Sep 2026)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generators)
- Foxhole shipped a convincing top-down war look with one modeler at the start. — [Sketchfab (Jul 2018)](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/game-studio-spotlight-clapfoot/)

### Inferences
Summary of routes (all figures are my estimates derived from the cited ranges, as of Oct 2026):

| Route | Typical cash cost | Calendar time | Strengths | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Full commission (mid-tier freelancer or Eastern European studio) | About $25k-$60k for a minimum cohesive Verdun set (2 factions, about 8 role variants, about 10 weapons, about 40 props, about 30 trench modules, 2-3 guns) | 3-6 months, 2-3 artists in parallel | Highest cohesion, you own the IP (with a proper contract), built to browser budgets | Cost, managing artists, revisions, contract hygiene |
| B. Kitbash / customize (CC5 + Export Licenses + MD + Substance + AccuRIG/Mixamo) | About $800-$1,000 tooling plus content licenses (store prices not retrieved), plus optional freelancer cleanup at $30-$80/h | Weeks per faction while learning, then days per variant | Cheap, fast iteration, period-correct base content already exists | Non-exclusive (others can use the same outfits), high-poly CC bases need heavy optimization, Windows-only tools |
| C. AI-assisted (Meshy / Tripo / Rodin / Hunyuan3D / Sloyd / TRELLIS.2) | About $20-$120/mo of subscriptions | Days for hundreds of prop candidates. Cleanup dominates. | Volume of props and debris, quick variation | No exclusive copyright on raw output, Steam disclosure, character topology unsuitable for animation, inconsistent style that needs a re-texture pass |
| D. Hybrid (recommended) | About $5k-$25k: commission the base soldier rig plus the trench kit, kitbash variants, AI for clutter | 2-4 months to a consistent vertical slice | Spends money where the camera notices (silhouettes, trench kit, animation) | Needs a firm style guide (palette, texel density, poly budget) before buying anything |

- **What a solo dev should not do:** pursue first-person-grade weapons, facial rigs or MetaHuman-class faces for a top-down camera.
- **Where to put money instead:**
  1. The base soldier silhouette, rig and animations.
  2. The trench, mud and crater kit and terrain materials.
  3. Artillery, smoke and VFX.
  4. A single global grade.

### Gaps
- There is no single source that prices a complete WW1 asset set. The route totals above are synthesized estimates and should be validated with 2-3 real freelancer quotes against a written spec.
- I did not research browser-runtime constraints (WebGL/WebGPU memory, glTF/Draco/KTX2 pipelines) or bought-pack availability. Other researchers on the team are covering these.
