# Realistic WW1 game assets: weapons, artillery and trench-warfare environment props (for a Babylon.js / glTF Verdun 1916 game)

Research date: 2026-10-03. Prices are list prices seen that day in USD, before tax. Fab listings often have two tiers, Personal and Professional; both are shown as "P/Pro". Fab data came from the live listing pages and from Fab's own listing JSON (the same data the site renders). "Verts" means vertex count and "tris" means triangle count, as each seller states them. Anything not checked on a product page is marked **(unverified)**.

## Q1. Weapons: Lebel 1886, Berthier, Gewehr 98, Chauchat, Hotchkiss M1914, MG08, grenades, flamethrowers, mortars

### Takeaway
Most WW1 small arms that matter at Verdun exist as cheap game-ready models. The low-poly Fab models by Manuel Rivas (Lebel, Hotchkiss M1914, Gewehr 98, each about 3k faces with 4K textures, sold as FBX/Blend) suit a top-down camera best, as does Ovidiu Barat's WW1-era grenade line, which ships GLB. Studio FieriaGold's "Weapon WW1" pack, Red Blue Pixel's "WWI Weapon Pack" and AmeliaTeale's low-poly packs cover whole arsenals, but they are UE-only. I found no game-ready WW1-specific flamethrower (Wex or Kleif) and no Minenwerfer on Fab.

### Inferences
- With an angled top-down camera, a rifle covers only a few pixels unless it is held by a close-up hero unit. The ~3k-tri Manuel Rivas models, or AmeliaTeale's under-4k-vert packs, are plenty; their 4K textures should be cut to 512–1K and compressed as KTX2 for the web.
- The FieriaGold "Weapon WW1" pack is the only single purchase that covers Lebel, Chauchat, G98 and MG08/15 together. It is UE-only with no LODs, so plan an export pass (see Q4) and per-weapon decimation.
- The M24-type Stielhandgranate models are WWII pattern. A 1915/1916 Verdun-accurate stick grenade (with a belt hook and a different head) would need modification or a custom model; this is a judgement from historical knowledge, not from a listing.
- A French "Schilt" or German "Wex/Kleif" flamethrower will probably need a custom model or a kitbash from generic tanks and hoses.

### Gaps
- Exact poly counts and texture sets for the CGTrader and TurboSquid MG08 and Maxim listings: the CGTrader search pages render client-side and could not be scraped, and TurboSquid returned an empty page.
- I found no commercial standalone Chauchat on Fab, CGTrader or ArtStation. Only free CC-BY Sketchfab models and the FieriaGold pack.
- I found no WW1 French/German flamethrower model on any marketplace I checked.

## Q2. Artillery: Canon de 75 mle 1897, 7.7 cm FK 96 n.A., 15 cm howitzers, 155 mm, Minenwerfer

### Takeaway
**Studio FieriaGold's "Cannons WW1"** ($39.99, UE-only) is the only pack found that contains both the **French 75 (75mm1897)** and the **German 77mm 1896 (FK 96)**, plus 150 mm 1902, 155 mm 1877 (de Bange) and others. It has no LODs and 20–30k verts per gun. **Formix** sells individual German WW1 guns (FK 96, 15 cm K16, 21 cm Mörser 10 and others) for $4.99 each, or 10 for $39.99, with FBX/OBJ/Blend/GLB/glTF included. That is the friendliest format for Babylon. Minenwerfer and French 155 mm models are thin on the ground and mostly limited to CGTrader, TurboSquid and Sketchfab.

### Inferences

- A historically correct 7.7 cm FK 96 n.A. (with gun shield and recoil system) versus the original FK 96 a.A. should be checked visually on the Formix and FieriaGold previews. The listings just say "FK 96" or "77mm1896".

## Q3. Trench environment kits and props, and the best "environment kit" options for most of a battlefield

### Takeaway
The standout is **Quixel Megascans' "Military Trenches" collection on Fab**, released 2025-12-18. It is **160 free photoscanned listings** under the Fab Standard License, each downloadable as **FBX, glTF, GLB and USDZ**. It covers modular dirt, wood and corrugated-metal trench walls, sandbag barriers, duckboards, ladders, stakes, obstacles, crates, burnt trees, shell craters, tunnel entrances, roof frames, barbed wire, ground surfaces and decals. A free UE sample level (179 meshes) shows how it all assembles. It is the only realistic kit that is both free and natively glTF.

The paid UE-only kits that best complement it are:
- **Katanalevy's** "Great War Trenches" and "Great War Trench Bunkers", $24.99 each: hero mud, wood and bunker pieces, but heavy Nanite meshes.
- **Red Blue Pixel's** "World War 1 | Warzone" ($69.99, 111 meshes at 164–23k verts) and "Trench Warfare of WW1" ($19.99).
- **Karamai's** long-running Sandbag Walls and Barbed Wire Pack.

For Verdun-specific concrete forts such as Douaumont, only non-commercial (CC BY-NC) scans exist.

## Q4. Are UE-only (.uasset) packs exportable to FBX/glTF, and which packs ship source FBX/OBJ/glTF?

### Takeaway
Yes, technically and legally. Fab's Standard License explicitly allows use "with any compatible tools (usage is not limited to Unreal Engine)". UE 5's built-in glTF Exporter can write Static Meshes, Skeletal Meshes, animations or whole levels to .gltf or .glb, baking complex materials to metal/rough PBR. The limits:
- It exports a single LOD, with no collision.
- Exports go through a UE project. Nanite-heavy packs will export at full resolution unless you reduce them first.

Separately, the Fab EULA requires you to "restrict end users from extracting" content, which matters for a browser game that serves glTF files.

### Cited Findings
- Fab Standard License summary: you may "Use the assets with any compatible tools (usage is not limited to Unreal Engine)". You "may not: Resell or redistribute the asset for free on a standalone basis". "Reference-Only tier will only grant you access to the content in form of a referenced asset, not its source format". — [Fab EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula)
- Fab EULA distribution clause: you may only authorize end users to use Content "solely as incorporated in the Project in object code and you must restrict end users from extracting or otherwise using Content outside of the Project". — [Fab EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula)
- Secondary coverage agrees that the Fab Standard License "permits commercial use in games… created in any CG application or game engine". — [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/11/epic-games-to-share-new-free-assets-on-fab-every-two-weeks)
- On Epic's glTF Exporter:
  - Static Meshes, Skeletal Meshes, animation sequences and levels can be exported to **.gltf** or **.glb**, from the Content Browser (Asset Actions > Export) or by Blueprint, Python or C++. — [Epic docs: Exporting UE content to glTF](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/exporting-unreal-engine-content-to-gltf)
  - Materials are converted by expression matching, falling back to **material baking** to 2D textures. Supported shading models are Default Lit, Clear Coat (KHR_materials_clearcoat) and Unlit. glTF "does not support Specular".
  - Opaque, Masked and Translucent blend modes are supported. glTF does not support collision geometry.
  - Only a **single LOD** is exported, and half-precision UVs must be switched to 32-bit.
  - [Epic docs: How the glTF exporter handles UE content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/how-the-gltf-exporter-handles-unreal-engine-content)
- Fab in Launcher (the Fab plugin) also offers exporting assets in FBX, USD or GLB, per Epic's documentation as summarized by search. — [Epic docs: Exporting assets from Fab in Launcher](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fab/exporting-assets-from-fab-in-launcher)
- **Packs that ship source or interchange formats natively** (per the Fab "Included formats" field):
  - All 160 Megascans Military Trenches items: FBX, glTF, GLB, USDZ.
  - Formix cannons: Blend/FBX/OBJ; the Collection description adds GLB/GLTF/USDZ.
  - Manuel Rivas Lebel, Hotchkiss and G98: FBX/Blend.
  - Red Viburnum Gewehr 98 and Corpse: UE + FBX. Red Viburnum Tunnels: FBX.
  - Akinaro Berthier: Blend/FBX/OBJ.
  - Ovidiu Barat grenades: Blend/FBX/OBJ/GLB.
  - MaksymF Lebel: FBX.
  - Dan Rose Trench Piece: FBX.
  - isoprotonic barbed wire: includes GLB.
  - Neo Star Low Poly Destroyed Buildings: Blend/OBJ/FBX.
  - Leartes WW2 Warzone Megapack: has a Unity version.
  - Paul N Fallen Soldiers: UE + Unity.
  - Sources: the Fab listings cited in Q1–Q3.
- **UE-only (.uasset) packs** include: Katanalevy (both), Red Blue Pixel (all three), Scale X, Sierra Division, Nameless Worker, Karamai (both), all Studio FieriaGold packs, EyeCandyAnimation (both), AmeliaTeale (both), AB3DX Lebel, Leartes WW1 Beach Warzone, Meshingun France Liberation and KitBash3D WW2 on Fab. — Fab listings cited above.

### Inferences
- A practical pipeline for UE-only packs:
  1. Install the pack into a matching UE 5.x project.
  2. Disable Nanite, or set a fallback, and generate or choose a low LOD.
  3. Export each Static Mesh to GLB via the glTF Exporter, with material baking at modest resolution.
  4. Post-process with gltf-transform: dedupe, simplify, meshopt or Draco, KTX2.
- Packs with channel-packed custom shaders (for example Karamai's AO/dirt in normal-map channels and the wetness MPC) or tri-planar/rain materials (Katanalevy) will lose those effects. They will rely on baking, which may need manual re-authoring.
- The EULA "restrict end users from extracting" clause is a real consideration for web delivery, where glTF/KTX2 files are fetchable. Common mitigations are Terms of Service language, packing assets into custom binary bundles, or light obfuscation. Legal review is advisable. This is an inference, not legal advice.

The EyeCandy listings show a "UE Marketplace License" tier rather than Personal/Professional, so their terms may differ.

## Q6. Synty POLYGON comparison (War Pack + WWI Map)

### Takeaway
Synty's **POLYGON – War Map – WWI** ($24.99, 98 prefabs) is a cheap, coherent, low-poly *stylized* WW1 battlefield set: trenches, craters, debris, barbed wire, tanks, planes, artillery, and British and German soldiers. It **requires the POLYGON War Pack**, which is about $99.99 on the Unity store per the search snippet. It ships Unity, Unreal and source FBX/OBJ. Its flat-shaded atlas look conflicts with the realistic or semi-realistic direction, and it has no French-specific content, but it is a useful prototyping or blockout kit.

### Cited Findings

  - Attachments: helmets, hats, gas masks (x14).

  - "This pack requires: POLYGON – War Pack".

### Inferences
- Synty is best as a **greybox and blockout layer** for an angled top-down camera, to validate layout and readability before buying or exporting realistic assets. Mixing it with Megascans in the final art would clash visually.
- Its very light polycounts and single texture atlas would perform well in Babylon. The realistic route needs aggressive optimization to approach that performance.
