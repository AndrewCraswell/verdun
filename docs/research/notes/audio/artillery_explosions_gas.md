# SFX libraries for WW1 artillery, shelling, explosions and gas (Verdun soundscape)

Research date: 2026-10-03. Prices are list prices seen on vendor/reseller pages on that date and change often (sales are
frequent on Sonniss and A Sound Effect). Licensing notes are brief; another researcher covers licensing in depth.

## Which libraries record real artillery (historic or modern stand-ins) with distance perspectives?

### Takeaway
Pole Position Production's **Warfare Library** series (sold via Sonniss and A Sound Effect) is the strongest buy for
real artillery: Vol. 3 "Heavy Bombardment" has real 70/105/155mm artillery recorded at both the firing site and the
impact site plus perspectives in between, with incoming whines/whistles, impacts and debris; Vol. 2 adds mines, C-4 and
"artillery grenade" blasts from 22 perspectives. Pole Position also recorded WWI vehicles for Battlefield 1, which makes
them the closest thing to a proven "WW1 game" artillery source. BOOM Library's **Cannons** gives real black-powder
mortars/bombards with 100 m ambisonic distance perspectives (useful layers, but pre-modern guns), and Krotos/Barney
Oram's **Explosions** gives real military explosives from 20 m to 300 m.

### Inferences
- No library found records actual WW1 guns firing live shells (French 75, German 77, 155/210/420mm). Every option is a
  stand-in: modern 70/105/155mm (Pole Position) for firing and impacts, black-powder mortars (BOOM Cannons) for
  low-velocity "thump" layers, and bulk explosives (Krotos, Tonsturm, Detonate) for big-calibre impacts. A 420mm "Big
  Bertha" impact would need to be designed by stacking the largest real detonations plus sub/tremor layers.
- Pole Position's recordings at the impact site with incoming whines are the scarcest material; most other libraries
  only record the gun end or the detonation.

## Which have incoming-shell whistle/whizz recordings and distant war ambience?

### Takeaway
Real incoming-shell recordings are rare: Pole Position Warfare Library 3 (incoming howitzer shells and rocket whines
recorded at the impact site) is the only clearly sourced high-end option. Bluezone's cheap designed "Artillery" pack
adds shell trajectories and distant barrages. Distant drumfire is best built from far perspectives (Warfare 2/3,
Krotos 300 m, BOOM Cannons "Distant") plus thunder/tremor layers, which is what DICE did for Battlefield 1.

### Cited Findings
- Warfare Library 3: "howitzers firing and incoming shell sounds", rockets firing and incoming; many weapons have
  incoming projectile whines and whistles, impacts and debris — [ASE search summary](https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-category/weapons/tanks-artillery); [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/the-warfare-library-3-heavy-bombardment/)
- Bluezone Corporation "Artillery – Designed Howitzer and Explosion Sound Effects": $23.50 on Sonniss / $24 on ASE; 154
  WAV files; 438 MB; layered artillery explosions, mechanical elements and distant barrages "combined with electronic
  elements" — [Sonniss howitzer tag](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects-tag/howitzer/); [ASE](https://www.asoundeffect.com/?s=artillery)
- Bluezone "Artillery" also includes cannon shots, distant artillery barrage, impacts, whooshes, grenade launchers and
  shell trajectories — [ASE (search summary)](https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-category/weapons/tanks-artillery)
- Bluezone "Tank – Explosion Sound Effects": 184 samples incl. distant blasts, incoming artillery shells and debris,
  from field recordings — [rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/bluezone-releases-tank-explosion-sound-effects-sample-pack)
- BOOM Cannons includes cannonball flybys (black-powder round shot, subsonic) — [BOOM Library](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/cannons/)
- High Impact Media "Explosion Volume I" (itch.io): 84 explosions in close/medium/distant plus 16 incoming shell whizz
  sounds — [itch.io](https://high-impact-media.itch.io/explosion-sound-fx-explosion-volume-i)
- Pro Sound Effects Detonate mentions "whistling effects" and "distant echoing decay" in its artillery category per a
  wiki listing; the vendor page lists no whistles — [fandom](https://soundeffects.fandom.com/wiki/Detonate_Sound_Effects_Library) vs [PSE](https://www.prosoundeffects.com/detonate)
- Warfare Ambiences (GameDev Market): 1.96 GB, 2h21m of soundscapes incl. distant gunfire and warzone battlefields,
  96 kHz — [GameDev Market](https://gamedevmarket.net/asset/warfare-ambiences-sound-effects-library-army-combat-battlefield-weapon-war-background-environment)
- Sound Ideas "Sounds of War": 75 effects, 74 MB, 24/48; historic WWII archival recordings restored with CEDAR, incl.
  full battle sequences — [Sound Ideas](https://sound-ideas.com/products/sounds-of-war-sound-effects-library)
- Articulated Sounds "Historical Firearms Shots & Battle": $45; 17 files; 0.36 GB; battlefield ambiences from Czech war
  re-enactments — [Sonniss artillery tag](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects-tag/artillery/)
- Historical accuracy of the whistle: field-gun shells (whizz-bangs) were supersonic, so near the target you hear the
  burst, then the whizz, then the gun; the whistle of subsonic shells comes from the shell's motion and rotating driving
  band, not an attached whistle — [Great War Forum](https://www.greatwarforum.org/topic/299289-did-shells-really-whistle/); [BOCN](https://www.bocn.co.uk/threads/ww1-whizz-bangs-whistling-shells.105009/)
- Whizz-bangs gave defenders almost no warning, unlike howitzer shells — [Great War Forum](https://www.greatwarforum.org/topic/53768-just-what-exactly-was-a-whizz-bang/)
- A Great War Forum thread collects audio of various artillery pieces (reference listening) — [Great War Forum](https://www.greatwarforum.org/topic/199181-audio-of-various-artillery-pieces/)

### Inferences
- Gameplay rule worth encoding: 75mm/77mm shells should arrive with a short supersonic crack/whizz and little warning;
  heavy howitzer shells (155/210/420mm) should get the long descending rush/whistle that lets players dive for cover.
  This argues for buying real incoming recordings (Warfare 3) and designing the long howitzer "freight train" rush
  from whoosh/flyby sources (Tonsturm The Whoosh, BOOM Cannons flybys).
- Distant drumfire bed: loop/grain far-perspective firing reports (Warfare 2/3 far channels, Krotos 300 m,
  BOOM Cannons Distant) under low-passed thunder/tremor (Tonsturm Tremor, BOOM Armageddon rumble), as DICE did with
  "distant rumbles of thunder".

### Gaps
- No dedicated, well-reviewed "incoming shells / whistles" library from a major vendor was found.
- Could not verify Sound Ideas Series 6000 or Hollywood Edge for WWI-era shell whistles (only "Sounds of War" WWII and
  the "Historical Series" with 437 effects surfaced — [Adorama](https://www.adorama.com/sound-ideas-hollywood-edge-historical-series-effects-download/p/sihehissr)).

## Which are best suited to procedural layering (separate transient/body/tail/debris layers)?

### Takeaway
BOOM Library's Construction Kits (Cannons, Urban Explosions, Armageddon) and Pole Position's multi-perspective
Warfare libraries (with mixing sessions) are the best layering sources; Battlefield 1's own approach was to split each
explosion into shockwave, environmental report, shrapnel flybys and debris raining down, which maps directly onto a
procedural Babylon/WebAudio system.

### Cited Findings
- Battlefield 1 (DICE: audio director Bence Pajor; leads Andreas Almström and Mari Saastamoinen Minto; external help
  from Pole Position Production and Criterion) — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/)
- BF1 explosions were layered as "shockwave, environmental report and debris raining down on the soldier"; shrapnel was
  built from recorded bullet impacts and flybys sequenced past the player; debris recorded by throwing dirt into the air
  onto surfaces, uniform and a helmet — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/)
- BF1 recorded at varying distances and in forests, fields, mountains, trenches, barns and urban areas (Sweden,
  California, Idaho) — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/); [iTech Post](https://www.itechpost.com/articles/53528/20161114/how-battlefield-1-crafted-its-sound-effects-to-world-war-i-accuracy.htm)
- BF1 battle ambience used "big world sounds" (one-shots at various distances) and "distant rumbles of thunder"; the
  dynamic mix let loud sounds duck quieter ones — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/)
- GDC talk on Battlefield 1 sound: "Redefining the Sound of the..." — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024111/Redefining-the-Sound-of-the)
- BOOM Construction Kits = raw multi-mic source; Armageddon CK has 2,800+ raw sources for building explosions,
  collapses and disasters from scratch — [Thomann](https://www.thomannmusic.com/boom_library_armageddon_bundle.htm);
  [BOOM](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/armageddon/)
- Warfare 2 ships Pro Tools/Reaper sessions with all perspectives aligned — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/the-warfare-2-library/)
- Krotos Explosions includes Weaponiser presets (layer-based weapon designer) — [Krotos](https://www.krotosaudio.com/?p=1020473)

### Inferences
- Suggested layer stack per shell: (1) incoming (whizz for field guns, rush/whistle for howitzers), (2) transient
  crack, (3) body/boom chosen by distance band (close/mid/far channels), (4) low-end thump/sub, (5) debris/dirt rain
  sized by calibre, (6) shrapnel flybys for close impacts, (7) tail/reverb chosen by terrain, (8) tinnitus/duck on
  near-miss. Multi-mic sources let distance be a crossfade between real perspectives rather than just EQ.
- For hundreds of shells per minute, a browser game needs short, pre-cut one-shots; designed versions are less useful
  than CK material because designed files bake in tails that will smear under drumfire.

### Gaps
- No public, detailed interview found on Verdun/Tannenberg/Isonzo (M2H, Blackmill Games) artillery sound design;
  only that Isonzo's sound design is praised — [TechRadar](https://global.techradar.com/nl-be/reviews/isonzo-review) — and that
  Niels van der Leest was Tannenberg's audio director — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannenberg_(video_game)).

## Mortars, grenades, mines, debris, tinnitus and gas: coverage

### Takeaway
Mines and grenade-class blasts are well covered by Pole Position Warfare 2; Minenwerfer-style low-velocity "thump" can
come from BOOM Cannons' mortars. Tinnitus is easy to design or buy cheaply. Gas is the weakest category: no gas-attack
library exists, so hiss comes from pressure libraries (HISS and a ROAR "Pressure") and alarms (rattles, shell-case
gongs, klaxons) must be recorded or sourced from generic bell/ratchet/siren libraries.

### Cited Findings
- Mines: troop, vehicle and tank mines; C-4; artillery grenade — [Sonniss WL2](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/the-warfare-2-library/)
- Black-powder mortars (Mortar 45, 66, 80) with impacts — [BOOM Cannons](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/cannons/)
- Mortar blasts in Detonate — [PSE](https://www.prosoundeffects.com/detonate)
- Debris: BF1 recorded dirt thrown in the air onto surfaces/helmet (technique to copy with own Foley) — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/); Warfare 2 "extended debris settling" — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/the-warfare-2-library/)
- Tinnitus: designed ear-ringing tones typically sit around 3–5 kHz; variants: steady ring, "blast ring" (instant
  onset with dull pressure), slow recovery — [Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/ear-ringing-sound-effect)
- A "Tinnitus Designed" library exists on A Sound Effect (details not retrieved) — [mirror of ASE listing](https://myshare-cms.siemens.com/ear-ringing-sound-effect/tinnitus-designed-tinnitus-ear-ringing-sound-effects-library-asoundeffect-com)
- Gas hiss: HISS and a ROAR "PRESSURE" (Tim Prebble): 2,700+ sounds from pressure devices incl. steam trains, pressure
  cookers, aerosol cans — [Sonic Field](https://sonicfield.org/making-of-hiss-and-a-roar-pressure-sfx-library-by-tim-prebble)
- Gas shells: came over with a quiet hiss and broke up on impact with a dull thud, often unnoticed; seasoned soldiers
  could tell them apart by sound — [Pritzker Military Museum (search summary)](https://www.pritzkermilitary.org/node/781); a diarist
  describes shells thought to be duds turning out to be gas shells — [State Library NSW transcripts](https://transcripts.sl.nsw.gov.au/node/100156)
- Gas alarms: hand-spun ratchet rattles (toothed wooden wheel, loud harsh clacking), bells/gongs from empty
  large-calibre brass shell cases, sirens, kettles, wooden clappers — [RMR Museum](https://rmrmuseum.com/gas-alarms-in-ww1/); [48th Highlanders Museum](https://museum.48thhighlanders.ca/item/gas-alarm-rattle-c-1916/); [Science Museum](https://collection.sciencemuseum.org.uk/objects/co154650/gas-alert-rattle-england-1917-rattle)
- Canadian War Museum discovery box sheet on the gas alarm rattle — [Canadian War Museum PDF](https://www.warmuseum.ca/s3/supplyline/assets/fwwdiscoverybox/FWW_A11_Gas%20Alarm%20Rattle.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gas shell = small burster "plop" + soft hiss, deliberately quieter than HE; this is a strong gameplay cue (players
  learn to listen for the absence of a bang). Design from small detonations (low-charge black powder, Detonate small
  hits) + pressure hiss.
- Gas alarm rattles and shell-case gongs are cheap to record in-house (a football/ratchet rattle and a struck brass
  shell case), which avoids licensing questions and gives period-accurate timbre. Whether French units at Verdun used
  klaxons/sirens specifically is unverified (see Gaps).
- Stick grenade (M24) and F1 "citron" detonations: use small/medium charges from Warfare 2 (grenade-class) and Krotos
  (small TNT/C4 weights); no library records these specific grenades.

### Gaps
- No library found specifically for WW1 grenades, Minenwerfer, or gas attacks.
- French gas alarm practice (klaxons, gongs) not sourced in this pass.

## Any libraries specifically marketed as WW1/WWI or "Great War"?

### Takeaway
Only BOOM Library's **World War I Firearms** is explicitly WWI, and it contains small arms only (no artillery,
grenades or explosives). Nothing marketed as WWI artillery was found.

### Cited Findings
- BOOM World War I Firearms: Bundle 571 files / 4,594 sounds / 15.41 GB; CK 499 files / 13.96 GB; Designed 72 files /
  1.45 GB; 24-bit/96 kHz; "from $139" — [BOOM Library](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/world-war-i-firearms/)
