# Paid sound sources for WW1 trench ambience, foley and human voices (French and German)

Research date: 2026-10-03. Scope: non-weapon soundscape (ambience, foley, voices, period mechanical). Prices are
list prices seen on vendor pages at research time (sales are frequent on A Sound Effect and Sonniss). Licensing is noted
only briefly; another researcher covers it in depth. Budget note: about 30 search/fetch calls were made. Several
vendor pages (IWM, Audiokinetic, SAG-AFTRA, Voquent, GVAA rate tables) returned 403 or were behind sign-up forms, so
some facts below come from search-result snippets and are flagged.

## Trench and battlefield ambience (wind, rain, distant war rumble, animals, whistles, bugles, flares, wire, duckboards)

### Takeaway
No commercial library found is dedicated to WW1 trench ambience at AAA quality. The realistic route is to layer
generic high-end nature recordings (wind, rain, mud, birds) with modern military-exercise "distant battle" recordings,
and to record or commission the period-specific items (Acme Thunderer and French whistles, bugle calls, Very pistol,
barbed wire, duckboards) as custom foley. Battlefield 1's team took the same approach: mostly original recordings.

### Cited Findings
- **The Warfare 2 Library** (Sonniss): 627 field recordings from a real military exercise, 22 perspectives (close,
  medium, distant), $199. Useful for distant war rumble beds. — [Sonniss search snippet](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/the-warfare-2-library/)
- **The Warfare Library 3: Heavy Bombardment** (Sonniss): artillery shells, impacts, distant explosions from
  howitzers, 70 mm and 105 mm artillery. Price not captured. — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/?p=36530)
- **WARFARE AMBIENCES** (Fusehive, via GameDev Market / itch.io): "hours" of battlefield soundscapes incl. distant
  gunfire, $149.99. Budget-tier vendor; quality unverified. — [GameDev Market](https://gamedevmarket.net/asset/warfare-ambiences-sound-effects-library-army-combat-battlefield-weapon-war-background-environment); [itch.io](https://fusehive.itch.io/warfare-ambiences-sound-effects-library-army-combat-battlefield-weapon-war-backg)
- **LYN083: Warfare Battlefields** listed on Shockwave-Sound; a snippet describes a set of 25 warzone ambiences
  spanning 4 eras including WWI (attribution of that description to this specific product is not confirmed). — [Shockwave-Sound](https://shockwave-sound.com/sound-effects/5273/lyn083-warfare-battlefields)
- **Thomas Rex Beverly** (A Sound Effect independent): 100+ nature libraries used on The Last of Us, CODA, Jack Ryan,
  Frozen II. Examples: High Desert Thunderstorms $45, Nature Roomtones: Deserts $80, High Desert Winds (gusts 15-50
  mph), Alaska Rain. Good source for wind-over-no-man's-land and rain beds; recorded in US, so check bird species. — [A Sound Effect: Thomas Rex Beverly](https://asoundeffect.com/sounddesigner/thomas-rex-beverly); [Alaska Rain](https://asoundeffect.com/sound-library/alaska-rain)
- **Battlefield 1 (DICE, 2016)** ambience approach: Mari Saastamoinen Minto used her own recordings from France for
  geographically accurate ambiences ("researching what birds and animals are found in that specific part of the
  world"), baked her own favourite rain recordings into rain, recorded debris by throwing dirt onto uniform pieces and a
  helmet; vehicles and planes came from Pole Position Production. No third-party libraries were named. — [A Sound Effect: Battlefield 1 sound](https://asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/)
- **Imperial War Museum** holds "Trench Warfare", a compilation of First World War Western Front sound effects and
  music (gunfire, horses, marching, orders, engines, German POWs, French civilians). The page returned 403 to fetch, so
  whether it is period audio or a later recreation, and its licensing terms, are unverified. — [IWM item 80000964](https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/80000964) (via search snippet)
- **ProductionTrax "Trench Warfare SFX"**: described as a historically accurate trench warfare ambience for
  educational and commercial use; price/quality unverified. — [ProductionTrax WW1 search](https://www.productiontrax.com/search.php?query=WW1)
- **Whistles**: the only "Thunderer" whistle SFX found commercially are plastic Thunderer whistle blows on Soundstripe
  (subscription), not the WW1-era brass/nickel Acme Thunderer. — [Soundstripe Thunderer whistle](https://www.soundstripe.com/library/sound-effects/32341)
- **Exceptional free options** (quality is amateur/variable): Freesound "World War 1 Trench warfare battlefield
  sounds" by paulbogush; Pixabay WW1 set (7 sounds). — [Freesound](https://freesound.org/people/paulbogush/sounds/345723/); [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/search/ww1/)

### Inferences
- Buy one or two premium nature-ambience sets (wind, rain, mud drips, crows/corvids, night insects) and one distant
  modern-artillery bed (Warfare 2/3) and build trench ambience procedurally in-engine; this matches what DICE did.
- Period whistles are cheap to buy (Acme still makes the Thunderer and reproduction "trench whistles") and record in a
  home foley session; the same applies to a reproduction Very/flare pistol pop (blank) plus a road-flare fizz layer,
  barbed wire rattle/cutting (garden wire and bolt cutters), and wooden pallet "duckboards" over wet soil. This is
  likely higher quality and more period-specific than anything in stock libraries.
- French bugle calls ("clairon") and Verdun church bells are better sourced as custom recordings or licensed archival
  recordings than stock SFX.

### Gaps
- Could not verify catalogues or prices for Pro Sound Effects, Articulated Sounds, Rabbit Ears Audio, Sonic Bat, Aaron
  Brown, Ultimate SFX, or "Ambience Kit" on WW1-relevant content within the call budget.
- No library found with dedicated field telephone (magneto crank/ring), rats, flies, dugout room tones, French clairon
  bugle calls, or Very pistol recordings; searches returned only generic items or AI-generated "flare" SFX (ElevenLabs).
- IWM archive licensing terms and whether its recordings are authentic 1914-18 audio are unverified (403).

## Military foley (mud footsteps, gear rattle, body falls, crawling, gas mask breathing, digging, sandbags, ladders)

### Takeaway
Generic high-quality mud/footstep and cloth/gear libraries exist; WWII-era gear foley is the closest period match. Gas
mask breathing and WW1-specific kit (canteens, Lebel/Mauser bayonet scabbards, entrenching tools) will need custom
recording or layering.

### Cited Findings
- **Firearm Foley & Mechanics: WW2** (Sonniss): 4,000+ sounds from 12 WWII weapons incl. handling and cloth foley
  textures; useful for rifle handling and cloth/strap layers on running soldiers. — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/firearm-foley-mechanics-ww2/)
- **1940 Military Gear** (Black Edge Audio, Gumroad): three WWII-era trucks plus 1940s prop foley (jerry cans, cargo). — [Gumroad](https://blackedgeaudio.gumroad.com/l/1940militarygear)
- **MOVES**: character foley clothing and gear movement collection covering Special Forces, Medieval Soldier, World War
  Soldier and others (publisher/price not captured in snippet). — [A Sound Effect foley category](https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-category/foley)
- Footstep collections on A Sound Effect / Sonniss: one with 3,263 footsteps across six surfaces and three footwear
  types plus gear/armour foley; another with 511 footsteps incl. mud and water. Names not captured. — [A Sound Effect footsteps](https://asoundeffect.com/sound-category/foley/footsteps)
- **Earth, Dirt and Mud Foley** (MuseHub): impacts, slides, digs and footfalls in dirt, mud and gravel. — [MuseHub](https://musehub.com/loops-and-audio/earth-dirt-and-mud-foley-sound-effects)
- Gas mask breathing: only individual tracks found (Artlist "Organic Tension - Gas Mask, Breath, Exhale, Inhale,
  Slowly"; Sonniss "Breaths" library, 223 files, not gas-mask specific). — [Artlist](https://artlist.io/sfx/track/organic-tension---gas-mask-breath-exhale-inhale-slowly/56520); [Sonniss Breaths](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/breaths/)

### Inferences
- A custom one-day foley session (mud pit, pallet duckboards, period-style canteen and webbing, shovel, sandbags,
  wooden ladder, crawling on wet earth) would cover most of the gap and give consistent perspective for a top-down
  camera, where close-mic detail matters less than many variations.
- WW1 gas masks (French M2/ARS, German Gummimaske) used filter canisters/pads; a reproduction or modern rubber mask
  through a cloth pad is a plausible substitute. Gas-mask breathing in period would be muffled rubber/fabric rather than
  a modern valve click.

### Gaps
- Did not find a dedicated "soldier movement" library priced/verified from BOOM, Pro Sound Effects or Articulated
  Sounds within budget; MOVES publisher and price not confirmed.
- No period-accurate gas mask recording library found.

## Human voices (French and German battle cries, orders, screams, wounded, gas choking, death; crowd charges)

### Takeaway
No commercial library of French or German WW1 soldier vocals was found. Language-neutral non-verbal vocals (pain,
death, effort, charge walla) are well covered by BOOM's Crowds - War & Battle and similar; language-specific barks
("En avant!", "Hurra!", orders) should be commissioned from native French and German actors. Budget per actor-session
is in the low hundreds of USD non-union, about $1,135 per 4-hour session at SAG-AFTRA scale.

### Cited Findings
- **BOOM Library Crowds - War & Battle** (Jan 2025): $329, 1,251 files / 3,935 sounds, 42 GB, 24-bit/96 kHz. Single
  male and female voice one-shots plus group recordings outdoors with a 22-channel mic setup; processed to varying group
  sizes, distances and intensities. Emotions: agony, moan, fear, pain, celebrate, fight, beg, death, war cries. The
  vendor-page summary mentioned "multiple languages" but this could not be confirmed on a second fetch. — [BOOM Library](https://www.boomlibrary.com/sound-effects/crowds-war-battle/); [search snippet](https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-library/crowds-war-and-battle)
- **BOOM Creatures Humanoid**: 15 male and 7 female vocal sets with pain (small/medium/large), death, attack,
  alerted, breathing; vendor says suitable for human combat as well as monsters. — [Rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/boom-library-releases-creatures-humanoid-sound-library/)
- **British Soldier Voices** (344 SFX): $64, 1,600+ files (800+ vocalisations), 435 MB, 24-bit/96 kHz stereo;
  whisper/talk/shout/grunt categories; phrases from SAS, Army, Royal Navy, Paratroopers, "modern and historical". English
  only, but the grunt/effort material is language-neutral. — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/?p=25416); [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-library/british-soldier-voices)
- **SonicArchive Military Personnel German** (Shockwave-Sound): 46 sounds of German military personnel shouting, only
  16-bit/44.1 kHz. Soundstripe has individual German drill shouts ("Achtung!", "Kompanie!", "Marsch!"). Thin, drill-ground
  rather than combat. — [Shockwave-Sound](https://shockwave-sound.com/sound-effects/12396/sonicarchive-military-personnel-german); [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/library/sound-effects/2870)
- Searches for a French soldier voice library returned none. — [search results, e.g. Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/product/army-soldiers-military-combat-warrior-voice-overs)
- **Battlefield 1** reportedly had voice actors wear weighted backpacks and play catch with cinderblocks to sound
  physically strained (secondary source; not confirmed from DICE directly). — [TV Tropes trivia](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/Battlefield1)
- Blackmill (Verdun, Tannenberg, Isonzo) is praised for authentic Austrian and Italian voice acting; no details of how
  it was cast or recorded were found. — [TechRadar Isonzo review](https://global.techradar.com/nl-be/reviews/isonzo-review)
- **SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement** (2025 deal after the strike ended July 2025): 15.17% immediate raise plus 3%
  in 2025, 2026, 2027; off-camera session up to 3 voices in 4 hours pays $1,134.95. Vocally stressful sessions
  (screaming, death sounds, battle cries) are capped at 2 hours and paid full scale. A Tiered Budget Independent
  Interactive Media Agreement exists for indie budgets. — [SAG-AFTRA tiered agreement](https://sagaftra.org/industry-resources/rates-agreements/tiered-budget-independent-interactive-media-agreement); [IIMLA PDF](https://www.sagaftra.org/sites/default/files/2024-08/iimla_0.pdf) (figures from search snippets; pages 403'd)
- Non-union: GVAA rate guide (industry reference, sign-up gated) cited by secondary sources as $200-$350+ per video
  game session, or $250-$350/hour; other secondary sources cite $300-$600 per 2-4 hour indie session. — [GVAA rate guide page](https://globalvoiceacademy.com/gvaa-rate-guide-2/); [VoxBooster 2026 stats](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voiceover-rates-statistics-2026/) (secondary, treat as indicative)
- Native French/German VO marketplaces exist (e.g. VoiceBros: native actors, commercial rights included, quoted
  $0.10-$0.25/word for narration; game bark rates by quote). Voice123, Voices.com, Voquent are alternatives. — [VoiceBros French](https://voicebros.com/fr/voice-actors/french)

### Inferences
- Plan: (1) buy BOOM Crowds - War & Battle for charge walla, death and pain; (2) commission 3-4 native French and 3-4
  native German male actors for scripted barks in period idiom (e.g. French "En avant!", "À la baïonnette!"; German
  "Hurra!", "Sprung auf, marsch, marsch!") with a historical-language consultant reviewing the script; (3) run vocal-stress
  material in short sessions. At non-union rates, 8 actors x 2 hours could plausibly land in the low thousands of USD.
- With hundreds of soldiers on screen, the top-down mix will be dominated by walla and non-verbal cries; language-
  specific barks matter most for the player's own squad and for officers.
- AI voices (ElevenLabs commercial, Replica) are possible for placeholders, but check consent/licensing of the voice
  models and SAG-AFTRA AI provisions before shipping; not researched here.

### Gaps
- No verified French WW1 soldier vocal library; no verified rate card for game barks per line from a primary source
  (GVAA and Voquent pages were gated or 403).
- No specialist game VO studio with WW1/military period expertise in French/German was identified within budget.
- No primary interview on how Battlefield 1 or Blackmill cast French/German soldier voices.

## Historic and period mechanical (horses, wagons, trucks, field kitchen, church bells, WW1 aircraft)

### Takeaway
Period aircraft are the best-served category: Pole Position's Fokker Dr.I (Le Rhône rotary) and Fokker D.VII
libraries are AAA-grade (the same vendor DICE used for Battlefield 1). Horses are well covered by BOOM Horses.
Period trucks and field kitchens need substitutes (WWII-era vehicles).

### Cited Findings
- **Fokker Dr.I 1917** (Pole Position, Sonniss): $449, 200 WAV, 8.09 GB, 24-bit/96 kHz; real 9-cylinder Le Rhône 9J
  110 hp rotary engine; 22 synchronised channels, 9 exterior perspectives (idle, taxi, landing, passes at various speeds)
  and 5 onboard positions; mono/stereo/surround/M-S. — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/fokker-dr-1-1917/)
- **Fokker D.VII 1918** (Pole Position): $449, 174 files, 7.44 GB; 6-cylinder 200 hp Daimler D.III inline engine;
  exterior takeoff, flyby, landing; onboard cockpit and engine. — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/product/fokker-d-vii-1918)
- Pole Position supplied planes, tanks, armoured cars and motorcycles for Battlefield 1. — [A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/battlefield-1-sound/)
- **Sound Ideas Bi-Plane Sound FX** and **Propeller Airplane Sound Effects** (1,701 sounds incl. biplanes, interior and
  exterior). Prices not captured. — [Bi-Plane](https://portal.sound-ideas.com/Product/1121/Bi-Plane-Sound-FX); [Propeller Airplane](https://portal.sound-ideas.com/Product/2473/Propeller-Airplane-Sound-Effects)
- **BOOM Horses**: 347 sounds, 9.0 GB, 24-bit/96 kHz; pass-bys at many gaits on wood, concrete, stone, mud, dirt,
  gravel, grass, sand; mounting/dismounting, bridle and saddle. — [Sound Ideas listing](https://sound-ideas.com/Product/178/Horses-Sound-Effects)
- Other horse options: The Animal Symphony - Horses (168 files, 717 sounds, 96/192 kHz, Khron Studio); Stallion (89
  sounds, 48 kHz). — [itch.io](https://khron-studio.itch.io/the-animal-symphoy-horses); [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/product/stallion)
- **Sound Ideas Sounds of War**: $129, 75 sounds, 24/48 max; remastered WWII archival audio (not WW1). — [Sound Ideas](https://portal.sound-ideas.com/Product/452/Sounds-of-War-Sound-Effects-Library)

### Inferences
- French aircraft (Nieuport 11/17 with Le Rhône rotary) can plausibly be covered by the Dr.I's Le Rhône rotary
  recordings, since the engine family is shared; the D.VII is technically 1918 and German, slightly late for Verdun 1916
  but acceptable for generic German aircraft.
- With top-down distant camera, one aircraft library is enough; buy the Dr.I first.

### Gaps
- No library found for WW1-era trucks (e.g. Berliet CBA on the Voie Sacrée), field kitchens, horse-drawn limbers, or
  Verdun church bells; Articulated Sounds and Pro Sound Effects catalogues not verified for these.
- No Nieuport-specific recording library found.
