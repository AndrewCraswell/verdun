# Professional SFX libraries for WW1-era small arms and machine guns (as of Oct 2026)

Scope: paid, professional sound libraries that hold real recordings of WW1-era weapons, or the same or near-identical
WW2 models, for a Verdun 1916 top-down RTS-style browser game. Prices are in USD unless stated. They are the list
prices shown on vendor pages when fetched (Oct 2026), and discounts change often. Licensing is covered only briefly here.

## Q1. Which libraries record actual WW1 weapons (or the same models in WW2 service)?

### Takeaway
Only one library is built specifically around WW1 weapons: BOOM Library's **World War I Firearms** (June 2025). It has
rifles, pistols, revolvers and shotguns but **no machine guns** and **no French or German-Army-specific long arms**
(no Lebel, Berthier or Gewehr 98). The rest of the period coverage has to come from single-weapon or WW2 libraries:
- **Vickers** gun: The Recordist. It is the closest commercially recorded relative of the Maxim/MG08.
- **Kar98k**: Pole Position, BOOM WW2, Superthump and others. It is a close proxy for the Gewehr 98, using the same
  7.92 mm Mauser action.
- **Springfield 1903**, **Luger P08**, **Mauser C96**, **Webley**, **Lee-Enfield**: several libraries.
- **M1917/M1919 Browning** and **BAR M1918**: several libraries.

I found **no** commercial recordings of the Lebel 1886, Berthier, Chauchat, Hotchkiss M1914, St. Étienne M1907,
MG08/MG08/15, Lewis gun, or the Ruby or Lebel M1892 revolvers.

### Inferences
- The Vickers and the MG08 are both recoil-operated, water-cooled Maxim derivatives with similar cyclic rates.
  Pitching or processing the Recordist Vickers is the most plausible professional source for German MG08 fire. A
  French Hotchkiss M1914 is gas-operated and strip-fed with a slower cycle, so it may need a custom recording or a
  slowed, re-sequenced M1919 or Vickers.
- The Kar98k uses the same Mauser 98 action and 7.92×57 round as the Gew98. Its longer-barrelled cousin will sound
  very similar at RTS distances, so a Kar98k library is an acceptable Gew98 stand-in.
- No French weapon is commercially recorded. Lebel and Berthier fire will need proxies: another 8 mm full-power bolt
  rifle such as the Mosin, Kar98k or Springfield. Lebel tube-magazine loading foley would need a custom recording.
- At the planned camera distance (hundreds of soldiers), mid and far shots plus tails matter more than exact
  model-specific mechanics. Model accuracy matters most for close-up UI and selection feedback, and for the
  signature MG sounds that players learn to recognise.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the exact Mauser and Enfield models in Watson Wu's *Rifles & Pistols of the World Wars*.
  Vendor pages list only "Mauser" and "Enfield".
- Could not render the A Sound Effect marketplace's search or category pages. They are JavaScript-only, and fetches
  returned only navigation. Small creators there may sell WW1 items that could not be found.
- No professional Lewis, Chauchat, Hotchkiss or MG08 recordings found. Absence is not proof, but nothing surfaced
  across several searches.

## Q2. Catalogue: price, contents, specs, perspectives, release, license, demos

### Takeaway
Shortlist by value for this project:
- **BOOM World War I Firearms** (Bundle $259 list): the core rifles and pistols, with close, mid and far mics, two
  acoustic environments and Ambisonics.
- **The Recordist Vickers Gun HD Pro** ($89): the only real Maxim-family MG recording, from attached mics out to 150 m.
- **Pole Position K98k** ($149, 24 perspectives, long and short tails).
- **BOOM World War II Firearms** (MG42, Kar98k, G33; 100 m AMBEO mic).
- **Superthump Weapons of World War II** ($55; budget M1919, BAR, MG42, Kar98, Luger, Springfield).

Watson Wu's *Rifles & Pistols of the World Wars* ($189) is a credible, film-proven alternative for rifles and pistols.

## Q3. Which libraries have distance layers and tails suited to a large battlefield heard from above?

### Takeaway
Best distance and tail coverage for period weapons:
- **BOOM WW1** Construction Kit: close, mid and far, two environments, plus Ambisonics and ORTF3D.
- **The Recordist Vickers**: up to 150 m.
- **Pole Position** single weapons: 24 perspectives, long and short tails.
- **BOOM WW2**: 100 m AMBEO.
- **Watson Wu**: close, medium, far and incoming.

For massed distant fire, add generic distant-gunfire beds: The Recordist *Gunfire HD Pro* (25 m to a mile) and
*Distant Gunshots and Explosions* (A Sound Effect / Sonniss).

### Inferences
- For a top-down camera 50–300 m from the action, the "far" layers plus separate tails do most of the work, with
  per-weapon close layers kept for the selected unit. BOOM WW1 and Pole Position supply tails as distinct
  perspectives, so tails can be cross-faded by distance in Babylon's audio engine.
- For MGs, the Recordist Vickers is the only period weapon with 150 m perspectives. BOOM WW2's MG42 at 100 m is the
  next best distant MG texture, though its ~1,200 rpm is much faster than WW1 MGs (~450–600 rpm) and would need
  re-sequencing.
- Ambisonic deliveries (BOOM) suit pre-rendered distant battle beds, but the game will most likely use mono and
  stereo per-shot layers.

### Gaps
- Exact metre distances for the BOOM WW1 "far" mic were not published on the page. They may be in the mic-setup PDF,
  which was not fetched.

## Q4. Vendor reputation for weapon recordings

### Takeaway
- **BOOM Library** is the best-known premium weapon publisher; its WW2 library is billed as the biggest WWII gun
  library.
- **The Recordist (Frank Bry)** is a long-running independent known for unprocessed, ranch-recorded guns.
- **Watson Wu** is a dedicated weapons recordist with film credits.
- **Pole Position** is known for exhaustive multi-perspective single-weapon libraries.

No independent forum reviews (Reddit, Gearspace) specific to these libraries were found.

## Q5. License notes for shipping in a browser game (brief) and notable free options

### Takeaway
All of these vendors sell royalty-free licenses that allow game use, priced per user or seat. **BOOM's EULA needs
checking:** it forbids "embedding into software or hardware of any kind" in its redistribution clause, while also
allowing videogame use. The licensing researcher should confirm how that applies to audio files a browser client
downloads. Free options exist but are low-grade or non-commercial.

### Cited Findings
- BOOM EULA:
  - Allows use "in any means and media… including videogame". Bars standalone resale and use in products that are
    primarily sound.
  - Its redistribution clause mentions "embedding into software or hardware of any kind".
  - Relies on user fingerprinting rather than mandated encryption.
  - — [BOOM T&C](https://www.boomlibrary.com/terms-conditions-regular/)
- The Recordist: single and multi-user licenses, "all sales are final". — [The Recordist](https://therecordist.com/product/gunfire-hd-pro/)
- Watson Wu: single workstation license. — [Watson Wu](https://www.watsonwu.com/weapons/rifles-pistols-of-the-world-wars)
- Sonniss: "Royalty Free Forever". — [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/weapons-world-war-ii-designed/)
- A Sound Effect: "Royalty-free – you can select the number of users on checkout".
  — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-library/rifles-pistols-of-the-world-wars/)
- Free and other options:
  - BBC Sound Effects (free site) is RemArc, non-commercial. — [Electronic Groove](https://electronicgroove.com/bbc-expands-sound-effect-library-to-33000-free-samples/)
  - The IWM holds First World War battle-sound compilations (archive items, licensing unverified).
    — [IWM](https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/80021020)
  - Pixabay lists ~1,300 "ww1 weapon" clips of unknown provenance. — [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/search/ww1%20weapon/)

### Inferences
- Because browser clients download audio files, the "embedding into software" and "no standalone redistribution"
  clauses matter. Per the project's asset pipeline, the safe approach is to ship processed, mixed or encoded
  derivatives inside the encrypted pack, not raw library WAVs. Defer final judgement to the licensing notes.

### Gaps
- Did not read the Recordist, Watson Wu, Sonniss or A Sound Effect EULAs in full for clauses on client-side
  distribution.
